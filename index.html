One thing to get out of the way first

iOS lets you force-quit any app, this one included. Nobody can build an alarm you cannot escape, and anyone selling you one is telling you something you will disprove on day two.

The actual claim: the alarm hands you straight to the verse screen, keeps its own sound playing there, and if you walk away it comes back two minutes later. It makes the wrong choice more expensive than thirty seconds of reading, which for most mornings is enough. That is a weaker promise than you expected, and it is the true one.

The part nobody says out loud

It was never that you didn't want to.

The alarm goes off and your hand is already on the phone, because the phone is the alarm. You swipe it away, check one thing, then a second thing, and then you are eleven minutes in and you need to be in the shower. The Bible app is on that same phone. It is four taps away and it has never once been the thing you opened at 6:41, because at 6:41 you are not making a decision. You are following a groove. The evenings run the same shape: a ten-minute scroll feels like ninety seconds right up until you look at the clock.

None of that is a motivation problem. You are not short on conviction — you decided years ago that this was worth doing and you have not changed your mind. What you have is a sequencing problem. The phone gets to you first, every single morning, and it does not need you to be motivated. It just needs to be first.

So the fix is not to try harder. The fix is to be first.

Most Bible apps are libraries: they wait to be opened, which means they get opened by people who were already going to open them. This one does not wait. It shows up at exactly two moments, and nowhere else.

Why out loud, and not a tap

Because a tap proves nothing. You can dismiss a verse-of-the-day notification with your eyes closed, and most mornings that is precisely what happens. Speaking is different in a way you feel the first time: you cannot say a sentence out loud while thinking about something else. The words have to go through you to come out of you.

It is slower and more awkward than reading, and both of those are the point. You do not have to take that on faith — there is a verse on the next page and a microphone in your device.

The fair question

"So Scripture is a toll gate now?"

The gate is a doorbell, not a debt. It has no opinion on whether you are a good Christian, it keeps no ledger, and reading a verse into your phone at 6:40 earns you nothing from God or from the app. Concretely, in the software: there is no failure state. It will never tell you that you failed, never scold you, never send a notification about a day you missed, never imply your faith is deficient. If you can't finish, there is a permanent alternative you can use forever with no penalty and no asterisk in your stats. If you turn everything off for a month, nothing chases you.

And this: if it ever starts to feel like law rather than invitation, delete it. Three taps, no exit survey, no "are you sure." Something meant to help you that has stopped helping you should be easy to put down — an app that made that hard would be exactly the thing you are worried it might be. This is a commitment device, in the same family as putting your running shoes by the door.

Your voice: there are no recordings, because nothing is recorded

  • Speech is processed on your device. Verify it the way the developer does: put the phone in airplane mode and the alarm still works.
  • Audio is never written to disk and never transmitted. What survives a reading is a pass or fail and a similarity score.
  • No account, no server, no analytics vendor. Nothing to breach and nowhere for your data to go.
  • Highlights and notes sync through your own iCloud, on your own quota, between your own devices.

One honest exception, and it applies to the demo rather than the app: the web demo has to use your browser's built-in speech recognition, and in some browsers — Safari among them — that routes a few seconds of audio to the browser maker's servers. That is a limitation of the web, not a choice, and it is flagged again directly under the microphone button. The shipped iPhone app does not work that way and never will.

Questions

The things people ask first.

Why not just use YouVersion? It's free.

Keep using it. It's excellent, and this isn't a competition for your one Bible slot.

But it waits to be opened, and the reason you're on this page is that the waiting isn't working. Every Bible app is a library and every library waits; the friction apps that do interrupt you — a countdown, a breathing exercise, a wall — interrupt the bad thing without substituting the good one. Also worth saying plainly: the whole Bible here is free too, permanently, so this was never a free-versus-paid decision.

What happens to recordings of my voice?

There aren't any. Speech recognition happens on your phone, raw audio is never written to disk and never transmitted, and all that's kept is whether you passed. There's no account and no server for anything to be sent to.

The web demo is the one exception, because browsers handle speech differently; that's explained on the demo page under the button and in the privacy block above.

I share a room. I can't read out loud at 6am.

The most common practical blocker, and it has a real answer rather than a workaround. There is a permanent silent alternative — trace-to-read, where you trace the words with your finger instead of speaking them. You set it up during onboarding and leave it on forever.

It is not a punishment path, it doesn't show up as a bypass in your own stats, and nothing in the app treats it as a lesser way to do this. You can try it on the demo page in the next thirty seconds.

Isn't this legalistic — earning something by reading?

Nothing is earned. There is no score, no ledger, no failure state, and no notification about a day you missed. The gate puts the words in front of you at the one moment you'd have missed them, then gets out of the way.

If it ever feels like law rather than invitation, deleting it is three taps and the right call.

Which translation? Is Android coming?

The Berean Standard Bible is bundled at launch. It's modern, readable, and it reads well out loud, which matters more here than in a normal Bible app. It's also in the public domain, so it can be bundled offline in full. WEB and ASV are planned as free alternates. ESV and NIV are not available — those require negotiated licenses a solo developer cannot obtain before proving the product works.

iPhone only at launch. No Android, no iPad layout, no web app, no Watch app. If you're on Android there's honestly nothing useful to put you on a list for yet.

What happens if I just ignore it?

The alarm comes back two minutes later, and two minutes after that. The shield stays up until the passage is read. Neither is impossible to get around — you can force-quit the app or switch the shield off in three taps — and neither will ever scold you for doing it.

What it does is make ignoring it more annoying than thirty seconds of reading. On most days, that's the whole difference.
