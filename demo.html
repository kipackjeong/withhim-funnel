Thirty seconds

Before your browser asks for the microphone, here's exactly what happens.

A verse appears on this page. You read it out loud, at whatever speed you like. The words light up one at a time as they're recognised, so you can see it working while you're speaking. When you reach the end, you're done.

This is the same thing the app does at 6:40 in the morning, minus the alarm going off next to your head.

What the microphone is used for

  • Only while you're reading. The microphone turns on when you tap the button and off the moment you finish or stop. There is no background listening on this page.
  • Only to match against one verse. The only thing being checked is whether the words you said line up with the words on screen. Nothing else is being listened for.
  • Nothing is stored. No audio is saved by this page, no file is created, and no recording is kept anywhere on our side.
  • No account, no email. There's no signup on this page and no field to fill in. You can do this and leave.

The honest part about this web demo

This page uses your browser's built-in speech recognition, and that is not the same as what the app does.

In some browsers — Safari among them — a few seconds of audio go to the browser maker's servers for processing. That's how the web works and it isn't something a webpage can opt out of. It's said here rather than buried because getting caught overstating this later would cost more than this demo is worth.

The iPhone app does not work this way. Recognition there runs entirely on your device, your voice never leaves the phone, and the whole thing works in airplane mode. That difference exists because of what a browser can and can't do, not because of a change of heart between here and launch.

If you'd rather not hand any audio to a browser vendor for a demo, that's an entirely reasonable position — there's a silent way through below, and you can join the list without doing either.

Done

That took about twenty seconds.

You just read Scripture out loud — possibly the first time today, possibly the first time in a while. Nothing was recorded. Nothing was scored. You said the words and the words registered, and now you can get on with whatever you were doing.

Notice what that didn't require. You didn't have to be in the mood. You didn't have to remember. You didn't have to decide, in the moment, that today was the day. A button appeared, and twenty seconds later there was a sentence of Scripture in you that wouldn't have been there otherwise.

That is the entire product. The app puts those twenty seconds in front of you at 6:40 in the morning, while an alarm is going off and won't stop, and again about ten minutes into a scroll, when your thumb has been moving longer than you meant it to.

It isn't built yet. One person is building it, it will take some months, and there's no date to give you that would be honest. If you want it when it's finished, leave your email — that's all there is to do here.
